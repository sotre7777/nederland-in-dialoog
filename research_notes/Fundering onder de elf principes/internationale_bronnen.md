# Internationale (niet-Nederlandse) survey- en think-tankbronnen over Nederland, gemapt op de elf kandidaat-principes

Scope note: this file collects only figures from international / non-Dutch surveys and indices in which the Netherlands appears (Pew, Eurobarometer, OECD, Edelman, Ipsos, Gallup, More in Common, eupinions, ECFR, World Happiness Report, Legatum). Dutch-origin sources (SCP, CBS, Ipsos I&O, EenVandaag, Clingendael) are included only where they turned up as the only available confirmation of an international figure, and are flagged as such. "Unverified" marks figures seen only in secondary coverage or chart-only pages that could not be read as text.

Mapping key to the eleven principles: INK = Inkomen; VNG = Vangnet; PEN = Pensioen; WON = Wonen; ZRG = Zorg; OND = Onderwijs; VEI = Veiligheid; IMM = Immigratie; LEEF = Leefomgeving/klimaat; BEV = Bevolking; DEF = Defensie/NAVO/internationale rechtsorde.

## Key question 1: Pew Research Center (2014-2026) — NATO, Russia, Ukraine, democracy, immigration, climate, meaning in life

### Takeaway
Pew shows the Dutch as among the most pro-NATO, most anti-Russia and most pro-Zelenskyy publics in its 37-country sample, but with NATO favorability sliding from 75% (2024) to 65% (2026) and satisfaction with democracy down from 76% (2017) to 58% (2024). On climate, the Dutch share calling it a "major threat" fell 11 points between 2022 and 2025, one of the three largest drops in the survey; on meaning in life, the Dutch are unique in how often they cite "freedom and independence" (highest of 17 publics).

### Cited Findings

DEF — NATO, Russia, Ukraine
- Favorable view of NATO in the Netherlands: 75% (2024) → 66% (2025) → 65% favorable / 30% unfavorable (Spring 2026, fieldwork Feb 8–May 13, 2026). Among 13 NATO member states surveyed in 2026, NL ranks 7th (behind Poland, Sweden, Germany, Hungary, UK, Canada). Question: "Do you have a very favorable, somewhat favorable, somewhat unfavorable, or very unfavorable opinion of NATO?" — [Pew, Views of NATO 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2025/06/23/views-of-nato-2025/markdown); [Pew, Global Views of Russia 2026 (PDF)](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/07/pg_2026.07.06_global-views-russia-2026_report.pdf)
- Favorable view of Russia: 9% (2025) → 13% favorable / 85% unfavorable (2026). NL ranks 32nd of 37 countries on Russia favorability (6th lowest). Same question wording as NATO item, applied to Russia — [Pew 2026 Russia report (PDF)](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/07/pg_2026.07.06_global-views-russia-2026_report.pdf)
- Confidence in Putin "to do the right thing regarding world affairs": 11% confidence / 88% no confidence (2026), tied with Spain — [Pew 2026 Russia report (PDF)](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/07/pg_2026.07.06_global-views-russia-2026_report.pdf)
- Confidence in Zelenskyy: 74% (2025) → 67% confidence / 30% none (2026); NL ranks 3rd of 37, behind Sweden and the UK — [Pew 2026 Russia report (PDF)](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/07/pg_2026.07.06_global-views-russia-2026_report.pdf)
- Pew 2025 NATO report (June 23, 2025, PDF) covers 2025 figures on NATO/Russia; the markdown page gave only the 66% NATO figure — [Pew 2025 NATO/Russia report (PDF)](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2025/06/pg_2025.06.23_global-views-russia-nato_reportc.pdf)

- "How satisfied are you with the way democracy is working in the Netherlands?" (very/somewhat satisfied combined): 2017: 76% satisfied / 21% not; 2018: 64/35; 2019: 68/32; 2021: 66/34; 2022: 64/36; 2023: 53/46; 2024: 58/41. - 2025 update: Pew's June 30, 2025 short read names NL among countries whose satisfaction did not change significantly since 2024 and places NL among publics "relatively happy with both their democracy and their economy"; the exact 2025 NL figure was not in the text (chart only) — [Pew, Dissatisfaction with democracy remains widespread (2025)](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/06/30/dissatisfaction-with-democracy-remains-widespread-in-many-nations.md)

IMM — Immigration (Pew spring 2018, published March 2019)
- The Netherlands is one of seven European countries where a majority said immigrants increase the risk of terrorism; opinion on "immigrants make our country stronger because of their work and talents" vs. "are a burden" was described as divided; on blame for crime the Dutch were split. The text page gives no exact NL percentages (figures sit in charts) — [Pew, Around the World More Say Immigrants Are a Strength Than a Burden (2019)](https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2019/03/14/around-the-world-more-say-immigrants-are-a-strength-than-a-burden.md). LEEF — Climate as a threat
- Share of Dutch adults calling climate change a "major threat" to the country fell by 11 percentage points between 2022 and 2025; NL, Greece and Italy had the largest declines among the 25 countries surveyed. NL is not among the three countries (Israel, Nigeria, US) where majorities do NOT call climate a major threat, so the 2025 NL share is above 50%. Exact NL percentages are chart-only (unverified) — [Pew, Global climate change as a threat (Aug 19, 2025)](https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2025/08/19/global-climate-change-as-a-threat/markdown)
- The 2022 baseline comes from Pew's 19-country "Climate Change Remains Top Global Threat" survey (Aug 31, 2022); NL figure not retrievable as text (unverified) — [Pew 2022 global threats](https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2022/08/31/climate-change-remains-top-global-threat-across-19-country-survey/pg_2022-08-31_global-threats_0-01-png/)

Meaning in life (identity / what citizens want to preserve) — Pew spring 2021, 17 publics
- Top Dutch sources of meaning: family and children 38% (rank 1 in NL; 9th of 17 publics, median 38%); material well-being, stability and quality of life 33% (2nd only to Spain, 42%); physical and mental health 31% (2nd only to Spain); friends/community 25% (tied 2nd with New Zealand); occupation/career 23% (10th of 17); freedom and independence 20% — the highest share of any public surveyed; society, places and institutions 18%; hobbies 10%; spouse/partner 8% (2nd only to US); nature 7%. Older Dutch (65+) cite health 38% vs 17% of 18–29 — [Pew, What Makes Life Meaningful? (Nov 2021, PDF)](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2021/11/PG_11.18.21_meaning-in-life_fullreport.pdf)

### Inferences
- The Dutch defence-consensus "from outside" is strong but eroding at the margin: NATO favorability dropped 10 points in two years while anti-Russia sentiment stayed near-maximal, which points to doubts about the alliance (and the US) rather than softening on Russia.
- The 2023 trough in democracy satisfaction (53%) coincides with the fall of Rutte IV and the November 2023 election; partial recovery in 2024 (58%) suggests volatility tied to governance crises rather than a secular collapse.
- The Dutch "freedom and independence" distinctiveness in Pew's meaning-in-life data is a candidate "sterk punt" citizens may want preserved, alongside material stability (33%, second-highest).

### Gaps
- Exact NL percentages for Pew's immigrant strength/burden (2018) and climate-major-threat (2022, 2025) items are only in charts/PDFs that could not be read as text; mark as unverified until the PDFs are opened.
- Pew data on trust in national government, religion/identity and economic situation for NL were not retrieved within the call budget. Pew's "economic situation good" and "trust government" NL series exist in the Global Attitudes toplines (2017–2025) but were not fetched.
- Pew 2014–2016 NL data (e.g. 2015/2016 NATO and Article 5 items) not retrieved.

## Key question 2: Eurobarometer (Standard and Special) — most important issues, trust, immigration, climate, EU defence

### Takeaway
Across Eurobarometer waves 2023–2026 the Netherlands is a consistent outlier: housing is named as a top-two national issue by roughly half of Dutch respondents (47–53%, vs. 10–20% EU), immigration and climate are named as EU-level priorities far more often than elsewhere, and support for the EU, the euro and EU support to Ukraine is at or near the top of the EU27. Trust in the national government (34%) lags trust in parliament (50%) and the EU.

### Cited Findings

WON / IMM / LEEF — most important issues facing the country (two answers per respondent)
- Autumn 2023 (EP Eurobarometer by Verian, reported Oct 12, 2023): NL vs rest-of-EU shares naming the issue among two most important problems facing their country: climate change 45% (EU 16%), immigration 30% (EU 14%), housing 30% (EU 10%) — [Verian, Immigration, climate change and housing in the Netherlands](https://www.veriangroup.com/news-and-insights/immigration-climate-change-and-housing-in-the-netherlands)
- Winter 2024 (EP Eurobarometer, fieldwork January 2024, >3,600 Dutch respondents; survey detail id 3218): housing a problem 53% (EU 20%); cost of living roughly one-third ("in line with EU trend"); immigration 16% (EU 13%). Local economy "very good" 20% (EU 10%), "good" 63% (EU 53%); quality of life "very good" 37% (EU 20%) — [DutchNews, Dutch more optimistic about economy and quality of life (Mar 2024)](https://dutchnews.nl/2024/03/dutch-more-optimistic-about-economy-and-quality-of-life) (secondary coverage of Eurobarometer; primary factsheet not fetched)
- Autumn 2024 (Standard EB 102, fieldwork Oct–Nov 2024, survey id 3215): most important EU-level issues per Dutch respondents: immigration 45% (EU 28%; only Cyprus higher at 50%), war in Ukraine 41% (EU 31%), environment/climate 35% (EU 15%). Most important national issue: housing 47% (EU 13%). Trust: national parliament 50%, national government 34%; EU trusted more than either (exact EU-trust figure not stated). Support for the euro 89% (EU 74%). Support for Ukraine: humanitarian 95% (EU 87%), financial 84% (EU 68%), financing military equipment 83% (EU 58%), sanctions on Russia 87% (EU 71%), EU candidate status for Ukraine 69% (EU 58%) — [DutchNews, Dutch more concerned about immigration than EU average (Nov 2024)](https://dutchnews.nl/2024/11/dutch-more-concerned-about-immigration-than-eu-average) (secondary; primary = Eurobarometer survey 3215)

DEF / EU — Spring 2025 and Autumn 2025 EP Eurobarometers
- Winter 2025 (fieldwork Jan 9–Feb 4, 2025; survey id 3492): 85% of Dutch say NL benefited from EU membership (EU 74%; described as the highest recorded since 1983); 84% want a greater EU role in protecting against global crises and security risks (EU 66%); 44% name reinforcing EU defence/security as top priority (EU 36%); 93% say EU countries should be more united (EU 89%); 75% say EU needs more resources (EU 76%); only 5% think people have significant influence on EU decisions (EU 13%) — [DutchNews, Dutch remain positive about the EU, back its role in security (Mar 2025)](https://www.dutchnews.nl/2025/03/dutch-remain-positive-about-the-eu-back-its-role-in-security)
- Autumn 2025 (fieldwork Nov 2025, published Feb 2026): 88% of Dutch want a stronger EU voice internationally (EU 86%); 92% want EU countries more united on geopolitical challenges (EU 89%); defence and security is the top area for strengthening EU's global position; EU membership positive 81% (+1); 72% pessimistic about the world's future but 84% positive about own/family future; top security concerns: cyber-attacks, wars near the EU, drones from non-EU countries — [DutchNews, Dutch say Europe should have a stronger international voice (Feb 2026)](https://www.dutchnews.nl/2026/02/dutch-say-europe-should-have-a-stronger-international-voice)
- Special Eurobarometer on democracy (survey id 3383, published Nov 2025): 67% of Dutch say "growing distrust of democratic institutions is a challenge for democracy" (EU 49%) — [DutchNews, Distrust and disinformation fears widespread (Nov 2025)](https://www.dutchnews.nl/2025/11/distrust-and-disinformation-fears-widespread-in-the-netherlands)

LEEF — Special Eurobarometer 565 Climate Change (fieldwork Feb 18–Mar 10/16, 2025; NL by online video interview)
- 65% of Dutch rank climate change among the most serious problems facing the world (EU27 47%); 63% took personal climate action in past six months (EU 59%). EU27: 85% see climate change as a serious problem (37% "very serious"); 81% support EU climate neutrality by 2050 — NL values for those two items not readable in the fetched PDF (unverified) — [Special Eurobarometer 565 Climate Change, NL factsheet (PDF mirror)](https://europokune.eu/dok/2025/EBSp565KlimShangh/EBSp565_ClimateChange_NL.pdf)

Earlier waves (context)
- Jan 2023 (Standard EB 98 coverage): 80% of NL residents worried about daily cost of living — [NL Times (Jan 2023)](https://nltimes.nl/2023/01/12/80-nl-residents-worried-daily-costs-living-eurobarometer) (unverified; headline only, article not fetched)
- April 2019: Dutch most likely of all EU publics to vote to stay in the EU — [DutchNews (Apr 2019)](https://www.dutchnews.nl/2019/04/no-to-nexit-dutch-most-likely-to-vote-to-stay-in-the-eu/) (unverified; headline only)

### Inferences
- Housing is the single most robust "outside-in" signal: in every Eurobarometer wave 2023–2025 the Dutch housing share is 2.5–4x the EU average, and no other national issue comes close. This is the strongest empirical anchor for the Wonen principle.
- The Dutch combine high immigration concern (45% at EU level, 2nd of 27) with very high support for Ukraine and EU defence; the two are not in tension in the data, suggesting a "control + solidarity" profile rather than a nativist one.
- Trust in government (34%) well below trust in parliament (50%) in late 2024 reflects the Schoof cabinet's weakness more than institutional distrust; combined with the 67% who see distrust itself as a threat to democracy, this is material for the "common ground" chapter.

- Primary Eurobarometer NL factsheets (europa.eu/eurobarometer) could not be fetched (not on allowlist); all Standard EB figures above are from reputable secondary coverage (DutchNews, Verian). - No Special Eurobarometer on social protection / pensions NL figures retrieved (PEN, VNG gap).
- Standard EB trust-in-institutions time series for NL not compiled.

## Key question 4: Edelman, Ipsos What Worries the World, Gallup, More in Common, eupinions, ECFR

### Takeaway
Ipsos' June 2025 wave ranks the Netherlands 1st of 30 countries on worry about climate change (28%) and 5th on immigration control (29%) and military conflict (25%), while only 21% think the country is heading in the right direction (5th lowest). Edelman 2026 puts NL at a Trust Index of 57 (tied 11th–12th of 28), with 73% showing an "insular trust mindset" and only about 1 in 10 believing the next generation will be better off. More in Common's only NL study (2020) found 66% worried the country would become further divided but 75% agreeing that people in the country care about each other.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Ipsos and Eurobarometer agree that Dutch worry about immigration and climate is high relative to other countries, while material worries (inflation, unemployment) are at the bottom of the 30-country ranking. The Dutch problem set is distributive/spatial (housing, migration, environment) rather than economic-survival.
- Edelman's 73% "insular trust mindset" plus a 1-in-10 belief in a better-off next generation is the sharpest external evidence of a pessimism/grievance layer under otherwise high institutional trust; it fits the 21% "right direction" in Ipsos.
- On defence the external and domestic polls converge: the Dutch strongly back NATO targets and EU defence, but personal willingness to fight (30%) is among the lowest in NATO; the principle wording "verdediging, NAVO, internationale rechtsorde" sits on institutional, not personal, commitment.

### Takeaway

## Key question 6: Polarization, perception gap and common ground in the Netherlands; international academic work on consensus culture / polder model

### Takeaway
No international survey or think-tank study specifically measuring a Dutch "perception gap" was found; the closest evidence is Dutch (SCP 2022: three-quarters think polarization is rising while measured opinion differences are not widening; two-thirds trust others) and More in Common 2020 (66% fear further division, 75% say people care about each other). Academic work on the polder model located is historical/institutional rather than opinion-based.

### Cited Findings
- SCP (Dutch government research institute, Dec 2022; two surveys March–June 2022, N≈1,900 and N>1,300): about three-quarters of Dutch think polarization is increasing and believe differences of opinion on social issues are growing; researchers see no clear increase in hardened opinion differences; two-thirds say others can be trusted — [NL Times (Dec 29, 2022)](https://nltimes.nl/2022/12/29/dutch-people-think-polarization-increasing-research-shows-otherwise) (Dutch-origin source, flagged)
- University of Groningen blog "Polarization in the Netherlands, part two" (academic commentary, 2022–23) — [RUG FEB blog](https://www.rug.nl/feb/research/frn/blog/polarization-in-the-netherlands-part-two) (not fetched; unverified content)
- SCP 2019: Dutch largely agree about Dutch identity; polarization seen as the biggest threat — [NL Times (June 26, 2019)](https://nltimes.nl/2019/06/26/dutch-largely-agree-dutch-identity-polarization-seen-biggest-threat) (headline only, unverified; Dutch-origin)
- More in Common 2020 NL: 66% fear further division; ~25% see country as more divided since COVID; 75% say people care about each other; 53% generalized trust; "Invisibles" (29%) are consistently 10–15 points more distrustful and alienated — [More in Common NL 2020 (PDF)](https://www.moreincommon.com/media/4qefbsy5/more-in-common-the-new-normal-the-netherlands-en.pdf)
- Eurobarometer (Nov 2025): 67% of Dutch see growing distrust of democratic institutions as a challenge to democracy (EU 49%) — [DutchNews (Nov 2025)](https://www.dutchnews.nl/2025/11/distrust-and-disinformation-fears-widespread-in-the-netherlands)
- Edelman 2026 NL: 73% insular trust mindset; grievance significantly higher among the insular group (51% moderate-or-higher grievance vs 17% among the open group; source slide hard to parse, verify before citing) — [Edelman NL 2026 (PDF)](https://www.edelman.com/sites/g/files/aatuss191/files/amsterdam/2026-03/2026%20Edelman%20Trust%20Barometer%20-%20localized%20Netherlands%20report.pdf)
- Academic/historical polder-model literature located: Huygens Institute, "The polder model: political culture in water boards (c.1200–c.1800)" — [Huygens KNAW](https://huygens.knaw.nl/?p=11349); Amsterdam University Press, "A Tiny Spot on the Earth: The Political Culture of the Netherlands in the Nineteenth and Twentieth Century" — [AUP](https://www.aup.nl/en/book/9789089647047). Neither contains survey data on value convergence.

### Inferences
- The pattern "perceived polarization high, measured polarization flat, generalized trust high (53–66%)" is itself the perception gap. International sources (Edelman, Pew democracy series) corroborate the mood side; the measurement side rests on Dutch SCP work.
- The best candidate for a non-Dutch "common ground" dataset is the European Social Survey and the European Values Study (NL in every round), which were not researched here.
