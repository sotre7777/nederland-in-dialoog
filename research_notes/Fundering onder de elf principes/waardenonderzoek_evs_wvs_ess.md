# Internationaal waardenonderzoek (EVS / WVS / ESS / ISSP / Atlas of European Values) over Nederland, als onderbouwing van elf kandidaat-principes

Scope note for the report writer: the objective was to find exact Dutch figures (with wave/year, question wording, URL) in the European Values Study (EVS), World Values Survey (WVS), European Social Survey (ESS), ISSP and the Atlas of European Values. Important structural finding about the evidence base: almost all published EVS/ESS/ISSP "topline" and Atlas pages present country results only as charts (images), and the text rarely states the Dutch value. The underlying microdata (GESIS ZA7500/ZA7503 for EVS, ESS data portal, worldvaluessurvey.org online analysis) require a login/online tool that could not be reached from this environment (the proxy blocked direct downloads, e.g. Our World in Data CSVs returned 403). So this file contains (a) the verified Dutch figures that do appear in text, (b) the exact question wordings and variable names needed to pull the rest, and (c) a clear list of gaps. Figures from Dutch national surveys (CBS, SCP) are included where they measure the same construct, labelled as such, because they are often the only text-published Dutch numbers. Anything that comes from my background knowledge rather than a fetched source is marked "(unverified)".

Mapping of principles to survey constructs (used throughout):
- Inkomen (wie werkt kan daarvan leven) -> EVS work values and work ethic; ESS "government should reduce differences in income levels"; ISSP income-inequality items.
- Vangnet -> ESS welfare module (government responsibility for unemployed; benefits make people lazy; benefit conditionality); EVS solidarity with the unemployed; CBS/SCP responsibility items.
- Pensioen -> ESS/ISSP "government responsibility: standard of living for the old"; EVS concern for elderly.
- Wonen -> ISSP "government responsibility: provide decent housing" (no Dutch figure found; see gaps).
- Zorg -> ISSP/ESS "government responsibility: health care for the sick"; CBS 2012 care responsibility items.
- Onderwijs -> no EVS/ESS core item found (see gaps).
- Veiligheid -> EVS/WVS confidence in police and justice system; CBS trust series.
- Immigratie -> ESS immigration module (R1 2002, R7 2014) and EVS/WVS immigration items; SCP trend figures.
- Leefomgeving -> ESS R8 climate module (2016/17); EVS/WVS "environment vs economic growth".
- Bevolking -> no direct EVS/ESS/ISSP item (see gaps).
- Defensie -> EVS/WVS "willingness to fight for country" and "confidence in the armed forces"; CBS trust in military series.

## Key question 1: EVS 1981-2017 and WVS 7 (NL 2022) Dutch results on work, solidarity, environment vs growth, confidence in institutions, willingness to fight, immigration

### Takeaway
Only a handful of Dutch EVS/WVS figures are published in readable text: willingness to fight (69% in 1990, 48% in 2006, about 52% in 2017/2022), social trust (44% in 1981 to 61% in 2017), national pride ("very proud" 21% in 1981 to 32% in 2017), and the finding that Dutch solidarity scores (concern for elderly > sick/disabled > unemployed > immigrants) were stable between 1999 and 2017. The EVS and Atlas sites confirm the question wordings and that over 80% of Europeans call work very or quite important, but the Dutch values for work ethic, environment vs growth, confidence in army/police/parliament and immigration items are only in charts or in the microdata.

### Cited Findings

Work / Inkomen
- EVS 2017: "over 80% of Europeans" say work is "very or quite important" in their lives; country results (including NL) are shown only in a chart on the EVS site, not in text — [EVS, About EVS: Work](https://europeanvaluesstudy.eu/about-evs/work/)
- EVS 2017 work-centrality battery (five statements, agree/disagree scale): "To fully develop your talents, you need to have a job"; "It is humiliating to receive money without having to work for it"; "People who don't work turn lazy"; "Work is a duty towards society"; "Work should always come first, even if it means less spare time". Young people agree less than older people with "work should always come first" in every country — [EVS, About EVS: Work](https://europeanvaluesstudy.eu/about-evs/work/)
- The Atlas of European Values (Halman, Sieben & van Zundert, Brill 2011) and the EVS 2008 Halman slide deck list the same work-ethos statements and a "civic permissiveness" scale (claiming state benefits illegally, cheating on tax, accepting a bribe; 1 = never, 10 = always), again without Dutch numbers in text — [Halman, EVS slides (HSE LCSR)](https://lcsr.hse.ru/data/2013/11/19/1334595181/Loek%20Halman.pdf)
- A British Sociological Association summary of EVS-based research reports that people were "as likely to want to work after the financial crisis in 2008 as before it" (Europe-wide; no Dutch figure in the search snippet; not fetched) — [BSA](https://es.britsoc.co.uk/people-as-likely-to-want-to-work-after-the-financial-crisis-in-2008-as-before-it-research-says/)

Solidarity / Vangnet / Pensioen
- EVS 1999/2000 solidarity wording: respondents rated on a five-point scale "to what extent they were concerned about the living conditions" of four groups: the elderly, the unemployed, immigrants, and the sick and disabled. In every one of 14 EU countries solidarity was highest for the elderly and sick/disabled, then the unemployed, then immigrants (deservingness: control, reciprocity, identity). Dutch means are only in Figure 1 (chart) — [Van Oorschot, Arts & Halman 2005, Policy & Politics 33(1)](https://core.ac.uk/download/420782436.pdf)
- Dutch EVS trend 1999-2017 (Sieben 2019, Religie & Samenleving 14(2), Tilburg University): concern for elderly, unemployed, immigrants, sick and disabled (1-5 scale) showed no significant change from 1999 to 2017 (1999 slightly higher than 2008 and 2017); concern for the elderly was highest and for immigrants lowest. Figures only in Figure 4 (chart) — [Sieben 2019, "Waarden, identiteit en levensbeschouwing: trends in Nederland 1981-2017"](https://religiesamenleving.nl/article/download/11575/13129/23667)
- Same source: Dutch social trust ("most people can be trusted") rose from 44% (1981) to 61% (2017), most of the rise before 1999; "very proud" to be Dutch rose from 21% (1981) to 32% (2017) — [Sieben 2019](https://religiesamenleving.nl/article/download/11575/13129/23667)
- Same source: social distance to other races, immigrants, Muslims (0-1 scale) was 0.07 in 1999 vs 0.11-0.13 in other waves; no significant trend overall — [Sieben 2019](https://religiesamenleving.nl/article/download/11575/13129/23667)
- Dekker & De Hart (2000), cited by Van Oorschot et al., found in repeated Dutch surveys no decline in pro-social attitudes, trust or supportive behaviour since 1960 — [Van Oorschot, Arts & Halman 2005](https://core.ac.uk/download/420782436.pdf)
- SCP Burgerperspectieven 2020|4 (COB/LISS, Oct 2020): 85% think many people are still willing to help others; 62% think most people can be trusted (12% disagree); 75% see a large contrast between rich and poor (earlier: 78% in 1987 Culturele Veranderingen, 72% 2008/09, 72% 2010, 75% 2015, 76% 2019, 77% 2020|1) — [SCP COB 2020|4](https://core.ac.uk/download/489783736.pdf)

Confidence in institutions / Veiligheid / Defensie
- The Integrated Values Surveys (EVS+WVS merged, OWID version 2025-06-27) carry the indicator "Confidence in the armed forces" = share answering "a great deal" or "quite a lot" to "I am going to name a number of organisations. For each one, could you tell me how much confidence you have in them: The armed forces". Wave years in that series: 1984, 1993, 1998, 2004, 2010, 2014, 2022. Netherlands is included; the data file itself could not be fetched (proxy block) — [OWID indicator metadata](https://archive.ourworldindata.org/api/v1/indicators/1076240.55ijpueyj9.metadata.json); source dataset [WVS/EVS trend file](https://www.worldvaluessurvey.org/WVSEVStrend.jsp)
- QoG datafinder exposes the same WVS variables (wvs_confaf "Confidence: Armed Forces", wvs_fight "Willingness to fight for country") for cross-national use — [QoG wvs_confaf](https://datafinder.qog.gu.se/variable/wvs_confaf); [QoG wvs_fight](https://datafinder.qog.gu.se/variable/wvs_fight)
- CBS national series (not EVS, but same construct, population 15+): confidence in the military 59% (2012), 62% (2013), 64% (2015-2018), 72% (2020-2021), 61% (Q2 2022), 68% (2024); police 68% (2012) -> 79% (2024); judiciary 69% -> 78%; House of Representatives 36.3% (2012), 53.2% (2020), 30.4% (2022), 31.3% (2024); politicians 39.7% (2020) -> 25.1% (2024); EU 39.2% (2012) -> 49.7% (2024); "other people" 58.3% -> 66.1% — [CBS 2025](https://www.cbs.nl/en-gb/news/2025/19/almost-7-in-10-dutch-people-have-confidence-in-the-military); [CBS 2014](https://www.cbs.nl/en-gb/news/2014/37/growing-confidence-in-the-military)
- CBS 2017 snapshot: police 74.5%, judges 72.9%, army 64.8%, other people 62.2%, civil servants 45.5%, EU 43.1%, Tweede Kamer 40.8%, banks 39.9%, large companies 39.4%, press 31.9%, churches 31.2% — [NL Times on CBS 2018 release](https://nltimes.nl/2018/05/28/dutch-confidence-police-least-church)
- SCP "Nederland in Europees perspectief" (2015), ESS Round 6 (2012): Dutch political trust (parliament, politicians, parties averaged, 0-10) "just above 5", near the top group (Denmark highest at 5.5); trust in police and the rule of law higher than political trust in every country, with Nordic countries above NL; social trust grouped with Nordics and Switzerland, clearly above most others — [SCP 2015](https://www.scp.nl/binaries/scp/documenten/publicaties/2015/10/08/nederland-in-europees-perspectief/Nederland+in+Europees+perspectief.pdf)

Willingness to fight / Defensie
- Question wording (EVS/WVS): "Of course, we all hope that there will not be another war, but if it were to come to that, would you be willing to fight for your country?" (yes/no) — [Clingendael Spectator](https://spectator.clingendael.org/en/node/5706); [Lane et al. 2026, Politics in Central Europe](https://politicsincentraleurope.eu/images/_PCE/2026/Lane_et_al_PCE_22_2026_1.pdf)
- Netherlands, share "yes" (DK/refusals excluded): 69% in 1990 (peak), 48% in 2006 (low after ~15 years of decline), slightly above 50% in 2017 and 2022 (52% latest). Decline across age groups levelled off around 2012; by 2022 respondents aged 60+ were among the most willing (authors link this to past conscription) — [Clingendael Spectator, "Who are willing to fight for their country, and why?"](https://spectator.clingendael.org/en/node/5706)
- Comparison: Germany 46% (1990), 34% (2006), 53% (2018), now slightly above NL; USA 79% (1990); Norway and the Nordics consistently high since 1981 (compulsory service); China 97% (1990) -> 79% (2013) -> 90% (2018), with a caution about political pressure — [Clingendael Spectator](https://spectator.clingendael.org/en/node/5706)
- Czech Republic/Slovakia for scale: WVS wave 2 (1990-94) 66.3%/65.5%; EVS 2017 48.5%/38.1%; WVS 7 34.4%/32.2% — [Lane et al. 2026](https://politicsincentraleurope.eu/images/_PCE/2026/Lane_et_al_PCE_22_2026_1.pdf)
- An NWA grant (2022, VU Amsterdam / Erasmus) funds Dutch research on willingness to fight, confirming this is an active Dutch research line — [VU Amsterdam](https://vu.nl/en/news/2022/nwa-grant-for-research-on-willingness-to-fight-for-one-s-country); [EUR](https://www.eur.nl/en/essb/news/nwa-grant-research-willingness-fight-ones-country)

Democracy
- SCP 2015 (ESS R6 2012): Dutch rating of "how democratic the country is" (0-10) = 6.9, 7th of 28 countries (Denmark and Switzerland about 8); support for democracy as a system 92% vs 89% European average (citing Den Ridder & Dekker 2015); perceived government effectiveness 6th of 28; life satisfaction 7.9 — [SCP 2015](https://www.scp.nl/binaries/scp/documenten/publicaties/2015/10/08/nederland-in-europees-perspectief/Nederland+in+Europees+perspectief.pdf)
- WVS 7 variable Q250 "How important is it for you to live in a country that is governed democratically?" (1-10) exists in the WVS7 codebook (IHSN catalog) — [IHSN WVS7 Q250](https://catalog.ihsn.org/catalog/12290/variable/F1/V283?name=Q250); the Netherlands WVS7 fieldwork was run via the LISS panel — [LISS data archive, study unit 498](https://www.dataarchive.lissdata.nl/study-units/view/498)

Immigration (EVS/WVS items)
- WVS7 contains Q34 "When jobs are scarce, employers should give priority to people of this country over immigrants" — [IHSN WVS7 Q34](https://catalog.ihsn.org/catalog/12290/variable/F1/V61?name=Q34); Dutch values not found in text.
- Reeskens & Halman-team publications on "Getting a grip on immigration: the citizens' view" (Tilburg) exist but were not fetchable — [Tilburg University](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/getting-a-grip-on-immigration-the-citizens-view/)

### Inferences
- The Dutch willingness-to-fight series is the single best-documented "value, not complaint" indicator for Defensie: a majority (about 52%) say yes in both EVS 2017 and WVS 2022, up from a 2006 trough, and in the same band as Germany; the fact that older Dutch cohorts are now the most willing suggests the value is tied to socialisation (conscription), not current threat perception alone.
- Dutch solidarity with the elderly and sick is highest and stable since 1999, which supports Pensioen and Zorg as shared principles; solidarity with the unemployed is lower and with immigrants lowest, which supports treating Vangnet (conditionality, "vertrouwen vs fraudebestrijding") and Immigratie as contested rather than consensus principles.
- Confidence in police and judiciary (about 75-79% in CBS series) is consistently roughly double confidence in parliament (30-40%), so "Veiligheid" is anchored in trust in the enforcing institutions rather than in politics.

### Gaps
- No text source gives the Dutch percentages for EVS "work very important", "humiliating to receive money without working", "people who don't work turn lazy", or "work is a duty towards society" per wave. These are in EVS 2017 variables v40-v45 (unverified variable numbers) and in the EVS trend file (ZA7503) variables C036-C041 (unverified); the Atlas of European Values site (atlasofeuropeanvalues.eu) maps them but could not be fetched.
- No text source gives Dutch EVS/WVS values for "protecting the environment vs economic growth" (WVS Q111 / EVS B008, unverified variable names).
- No text source gives Dutch EVS/WVS confidence in army/police/parliament by wave; only the CBS national series (2012-2024) was available in text.
- WVS 7 Netherlands (2022, LISS) results for importance of democracy, immigration items (Q121-Q130, unverified) and willingness to fight (Q151, unverified) could not be read; only the Clingendael summary (52%) was available.
- The Sieben 2019 Tilburg article covers trust, pride, solidarity, religion and tolerance but not work ethic, environment, institutions, democracy or income equality.

## Key question 2: ESS rounds 1-11 Netherlands (welfare 2008/2016, immigration 2002/2014, climate 2016, democracy 2012/2020)

### Takeaway
The ESS topline reports give exact question wordings and European averages, but Dutch values appear only in charts, with three exceptions: the ESS Round 8 climate module (NL: 96.2% say the climate is changing, 91.8% say it is at least partly human-caused, 61.6% expect bad impacts), SCP's reading of ESS Round 6 (democracy 6.9/10, political trust just above 5, about 80% want "a few" or "some" immigrants of other ethnic groups) and the Round 7 immigration report's description of NL as "middling" in both 2002 and 2014. National SCP/CBS series fill some holes (two-thirds want smaller income gaps, 2006; over 90% see care for the elderly as at least partly a government task, 2012).

### Cited Findings

Welfare attitudes (ESS R4 2008, R8 2016) — Vangnet, Pensioen, Zorg, Inkomen
- ESS R8 (2016) wording: "the government's responsibility to ensure a reasonable standard of living for the old" (0-10); averages exceed 8 in most countries outside Western Europe; NL plotted in Figure 1 but value not given. Support for government provision for the elderly fell in 16 of 20 countries since 2008 (NL not named either way) — [ESS Topline 8, Welfare](https://www.europeansocialsurvey.org/sites/default/files/2023-06/TL8-Welfare-English.pdf)
- ESS R8: government responsibility for the unemployed (0-10) ranges 5.9-7.7 across countries; NL value only in Figure 3 — [ESS Topline 8](https://www.europeansocialsurvey.org/sites/default/files/2023-06/TL8-Welfare-English.pdf)
- ESS R8 benefit conditionality item: what should happen to unemployment benefits if a person turns down a job paying much less than their previous one (0 = keep all, 3 = lose all); NL not named — [ESS Topline 8](https://www.europeansocialsurvey.org/sites/default/files/2023-06/TL8-Welfare-English.pdf)
- ESS R8 basic income: for/against a scheme paying everyone a monthly income covering essential living costs, replacing many other benefits; range 33.9% (Norway) to 80.4% (Lithuania); NL value only in chart — [ESS Topline 8](https://www.europeansocialsurvey.org/sites/default/files/2023-06/TL8-Welfare-English.pdf)
- ESS R8 immigrants' welfare rights (Europe-wide): 9.7% never, 9.0% immediately, 29.1% upon citizenship, 43.1% after working and paying taxes at least a year; NL in chart only — [ESS Topline 8](https://www.europeansocialsurvey.org/sites/default/files/2023-06/TL8-Welfare-English.pdf)
- ESS R4 (2008) topline: lowest government-responsibility index values are in "some of the Western European countries"; Nordics and Netherlands cluster top-right on perceived government quality (expert vs public, R = 0.81); no NL numbers — [ESS Topline 2, Welfare attitudes in Europe](https://www.europeansocialsurvey.org/sites/default/files/2023-06/ESS4_toplines_issue_2_welfare_attitudes_in_europe.pdf)
- ESS R8 welfare module questionnaire template (full wording of all items incl. gvslvol, gvslvue, gvhlthc, sbstrec, sblazy; variable names unverified) — [ESS8 welfare module template](https://www.europeansocialsurvey.org/sites/default/files/2023-06/ESS8_welfare_final_module_template.pdf)
- ESS R10 (2022, pooled 30 countries, n = 37,610, NL not in the VoxDash list): "Government should reduce differences in income levels": agree strongly 31.7%, agree 42.1%, neither 16.2%, disagree 7.8%, disagree strongly 2.2% (about 74% agree) — [VoxDash ESS10 gincdif](https://www.voxdash.com/question/154227)
- ESS R10 (pooled) perceived reality item "In [country] the government takes measures to reduce differences in income levels" (0 = does not apply at all, 10 = applies completely): modal answer 5 (15.6%); 11.1% at 0 — [VoxDash ESS10](https://www.voxdash.com/question/154344)
- Dutch national substitute (CBS, National Voters Survey 2006): nearly two-thirds of voters favour reducing income gaps; one in five want them unchanged; 13% want them larger; 7 in 10 women vs 6 in 10 men; over 55% of 18-25s vs about 70% of 45+; three-quarters of lower-educated vs 60% of higher-educated — [CBS 2007](https://www.cbs.nl/en-gb/news/2007/41/majority-of-dutch-population-would-like-to-see-income-gaps-narrowed)
- Dutch national substitute (CBS survey early 2012, 18+): income for disabled people fully government responsibility 57%, partly about 40%, not 3%; care for older people: almost everyone at least partly, just over half entirely a government responsibility; income for the unemployed: 35% fully, 54% partly; over 80% think government should help people find jobs and provide enough childcare; about 87% say it should enable care leave — [CBS 2013](https://www.cbs.nl/en-gb/news/2013/15/population-thinks-government-should-take-care-of-vulnerable-groups)

Immigration (ESS R1 2002, R7 2014) — Immigratie
- ESS R7 wording: "Is [country] made a worse or better place to live by people coming to live here from other countries?" (0-10); "cultural life undermined or enriched"; "take jobs away or help create new jobs"; "take out more than they put in (taxes/services)"; allow "many / some / a few / none" immigrants of same race, different race, poorer countries outside Europe — [ESS Topline 7, Immigration](https://www.europeansocialsurvey.org/sites/default/files/2023-06/TL7-Immigration-English.pdf)
- ESS R7: Netherlands described as having "middling views" on better/worse place to live in both 2002 and 2014 (with Germany and Spain); NL is not among countries where polarisation by age/education grew most (Austria, Finland, Spain, Sweden, UK, Hungary, Poland, Slovenia); NL foreign-born growth 2003-2013 below 20% — [ESS Topline 7](https://www.europeansocialsurvey.org/sites/default/files/2023-06/TL7-Immigration-English.pdf)
- ESS R9 (2018/19, 19-country release): average 5.1 on immigration good/bad for economy (0-10) and 5.3 on cultural life undermined/enriched; most countries rate the cultural contribution higher than the economic one; NL not reported in the text — [Quartz, on ESS R9](https://qz.com/1775059/data-shows-what-europeans-think-of-immigrants)
- SCP 2015 (ESS R6 2012): most Dutch favour admitting only a limited number of migrants of other ethnic groups; about 80% say "a few" or "some"; similar to Denmark, Finland and Switzerland — [SCP 2015](https://www.scp.nl/binaries/scp/documenten/publicaties/2015/10/08/nederland-in-europees-perspectief/Nederland+in+Europees+perspectief.pdf)
- SCP 2015 Dutch trend (national surveys): negative view of migrants' share 32% (down from about 50% since 2002); objection to a neighbour of another ethnic background 33% (down from almost 60%, 2002-2013); objection to a son-in-law of another ethnic background 58% (down from 68%, 2004-2013) — [SCP 2015](https://www.scp.nl/binaries/scp/documenten/publicaties/2015/10/08/nederland-in-europees-perspectief/Nederland+in+Europees+perspectief.pdf)
- Earlier ESS (2002-2008) country trend on "immigrants are bad for the economy" is published with country lines in the Swiss Social Report (NL included in chart, no text value) — [Social Report 2016](https://socialreport.ch/chapter-2/opinions-on-foreigners/immigrants-are-bad-for-the-economy-development-by-country-2002-2008.html?lang=en)

Climate (ESS R8 2016/17) — Leefomgeving
- NL: 96.2% think the world's climate is "probably" or "definitely" changing (8th highest of 23); 91.8% say climate change is at least partly caused by human activity (joint 13th-14th with Austria); 61.6% expect impacts to be bad (scores 0-4 on 0 = extremely bad to 10 = extremely good; 3rd lowest, only Israel and Estonia lower) — [ESS Topline 9, Climate Change](https://www.europeansocialsurvey.org/sites/default/files/2023-06/TL9_Climate-Change-English.pdf)
- EU/EFTA average from the same round: 73.4% strongly or somewhat in favour of subsidising renewable energy — [City St George's on ESS R8](https://www.citystgeorges.ac.uk/news-and-events/news/2017/11/europeans-reveal-views-on-climate-change-in-latest-round-of-the-european-social-survey)
- NL values for worry about climate change, energy affordability, personal responsibility, fossil-fuel taxes, subsidies and appliance bans appear only in Figures 1-6 of the topline — [ESS Topline 9](https://www.europeansocialsurvey.org/sites/default/files/2023-06/TL9_Climate-Change-English.pdf)
- Dutch national substitute (Publieksmonitor Klimaat en Energie 2021, Motivaction for EZK, n = 2,452, 18-80): 57% worried about the current state of the climate; 59% about the world left to future generations; 70% see large companies and 66% the government as responsible for tackling it; 41% feel responsible for CO2 emissions, 25% do not; seven in ten see humans as (partly) the cause — [Duurzaamnieuws](https://duurzaamnieuws.nl/bedrijven-en-overheid-moeten-klimaatproblemen-oplossen-vinden-nederlanders)
- CBS 2023: "three-quarters of Dutch adults worry about impact of climate change" (headline only; not fetched) — [CBS 2023](https://www.cbs.nl/en-gb/news/2023/48/three-quarters-of-dutch-adults-worry-about-impact-of-climate-change)

Democracy (ESS R6 2012, R10 2020/22)
- See SCP 2015 findings under Key question 1 (6.9/10 democratic rating, 7th of 28; political trust just above 5; 92% support democracy) — [SCP 2015](https://www.scp.nl/binaries/scp/documenten/publicaties/2015/10/08/nederland-in-europees-perspectief/Nederland+in+Europees+perspectief.pdf)
- SCP COB (Oct 2020): 66% give the national government a sufficient trust rating, down from April 2020 but well above pre-crisis levels; 75% prefer a government that decides what is allowed and fines (vs 10% personal responsibility) during the pandemic — [SCP COB 2020|4](https://core.ac.uk/download/489783736.pdf)

### Inferences
- On Leefomgeving the Dutch are among the most convinced in Europe that climate change is real and human-caused, but relatively pessimistic about impacts; combined with the national finding that two-thirds assign responsibility to government and large companies, this reads as a shared value ("het milieu moet beschermd worden") with disagreement mainly about who pays.
- On Inkomen/Vangnet, the pooled ESS R10 figure (about 74% agree government should reduce income differences) and the Dutch 2006 figure (nearly two-thirds) suggest the Netherlands sits at or slightly below the European average on redistribution preference (unverified for NL specifically; the Dutch ESS gincdif value needs the microdata).
- On Immigratie, "middling" ESS positions and the SCP-documented decline in social-distance objections point to an attitude that is neither hostile nor open-door: around 80% want "some" or "a few" migrants, i.e. the shared value is control of tempo rather than closure.

### Gaps
- Dutch per-round values for ESS gincdif (reduce income differences), gvslvol/gvslvue/gvhlthc (government responsibility old/unemployed/health), sblazy ("social benefits make people lazy"), imbgeco/imueclt/imwbcnt (immigration economy/culture/place to live) and imsmetn/imdfetn/impcntr (allow many/few) could not be obtained: topline PDFs show them only as charts and VoxDash excludes NL. Variable names given from memory (unverified). The ESS Data Portal (ess.sikt.no) requires registration.
- ESS R10 democracy module (2020/22) Dutch results not found in text.
- Climate module NL values for worry, taxes, subsidies were not in text.

## Key question 3: ISSP Role of Government (1985-2016) and Social Inequality, Dutch results

### Takeaway
No Dutch ISSP Role of Government percentages were found in text. The ISSP 2016 Role of Government V (GESIS ZA6900) record does not list the Netherlands among participating countries, and the Dutch distributions for earlier waves (1990, 1996, 2006) are only in the GESIS datasets. The Dutch CBS 2012 survey asks near-identical "government responsibility" items and is the best text-published substitute.

### Cited Findings
- ISSP 2016 Role of Government V (GESIS ZA6900, v2.0.0, doi:10.4232/1.13052) variable v33 "Q8b Who should provide: care for older people" (answers include government, private companies, non-profits, family); NL not in the record's country list — [GESIS exploredata ZA6900 v33](https://data.gesis.org/gesiskg/resource/exploredata-ZA6900_Varv33); study record — [CESSDA/OpenPolar ISSP 2016](https://openpolar.no/Record/ftcessda:2c15c746d703cd6fcfa431aef0f3d2022acf8c22381b3e2a77813ba9f3afa021)

## Key question 4: Academic syntheses on Dutch value stability/change (Inglehart, Halman/Sieben/van Zundert, Tilburg EVS team, SCP "Nederland in Europa")

### Takeaway
The Tilburg EVS team (Halman, Sieben, Reeskens) and SCP consistently describe Dutch values as stable or slowly liberalising since 1981: rising social trust and national pride, stable solidarity and tolerance, high and stable support for democracy, and a Dutch position close to the Nordic countries on trust and satisfaction. Inglehart-style syntheses place the Netherlands at the "secular-rational / self-expression" extreme, but no fetched source gave the Dutch index coordinates.

### Cited Findings
- Sieben, I. (2019), "Waarden, identiteit en levensbeschouwing: trends in Nederland 1981-2017", Religie & Samenleving 14(2): uses all five Dutch EVS waves (1981, 1990, 1999, 2008, 2017; Table 1 gives modes and n); reports stable solidarity 1999-2017, rising trust (44% -> 61%), rising pride (21% -> 32% very proud), no trend in social distance, secularisation (2017: 11% atheist, 24% no God or life force) — [Sieben 2019](https://religiesamenleving.nl/article/download/11575/13129/23667)
- EVS results for the Netherlands were presented at the Religie & Samenleving symposium, Tilburg, 13 June 2022: Inge Sieben on Dutch value trends 1981-2017, Tim Reeskens on value polarisation between educational groups; links to atlasofeuropeanvalues.eu and juniorkennisbank.nl — [EVS news](https://europeanvaluesstudy.eu/presentation-of-evs-results-in-the-netherlands/)
- Halman & Sieben (2011), "Respect man!" (reviewed via an interview in Religie & Samenleving 7(2), 2012): Dutch life satisfaction about 7.5-8 regardless of income, only half a point between highest and lowest income quartile; civic involvement high including among the young; lower-educated, older people, Muslims and Protestants less tolerant on homosexuality, abortion, euthanasia — [Hak & Nauta 2012, "In gesprek met Loek Halman"](https://religiesamenleving.nl/article/download/12968/14528/27102)

- SCP (2015), "Nederland in Europees perspectief": NL scores high on democracy rating (6.9, 7th/28), political trust (just above 5), social trust, life satisfaction (7.9, only Denmark clearly higher), government effectiveness (6th/28); on immigration about 80% want "a few" or "some" migrants of other ethnic groups — [SCP 2015](https://www.scp.nl/binaries/scp/documenten/publicaties/2015/10/08/nederland-in-europees-perspectief/Nederland+in+Europees+perspectief.pdf)

### Inferences
- Across sources the Dutch profile is: high interpersonal and institutional (police/judiciary) trust, stable solidarity hierarchy, high democratic support, moderate and stable immigration attitudes, and strong environmental concern. This is the empirical basis for claiming that the eleven principles are values with decades of stability behind them, with Immigratie and Vangnet-conditionality as the two areas where the survey literature shows the most internal division.
- The Tilburg team's emphasis on polarisation by education (Reeskens 2022) is the main caveat: averages can hide a growing gap between lower- and higher-educated citizens, which is directly relevant to a framework aimed at schools and municipalities.

### Gaps
